Tensorial Formulation of Backpropagation

The matrix formulation of both feed-foward neural networks and convolutional neural networks (and other networks as well, for example, transformers) is inspired by the linear relationship between the parameters of the network. However, when the dimensions of the space the parameters live in begins to grow, and so do the relationships between parameters, matrices become an inappropriate tool to mathematically describe the model and a rather clumsy tool to describe the backpropogation algorithm, so here, tensors are chosen instead.

1. Feed Forward Neural Networks

We will call a neural network a layered model that produces features of samples in a "linear" way, by using parameters and the sample features at previous layers and then applying a non-linear transform. Define the following:
  • Dataset: $$\mathcal{D} = \{(\{\bar{x}^{i,j}\}_{j \in J}, \bar{v}^i)\}_{i \in I} $$ for some index set $I$, where $x^{i,j} \in \mathbb{R}^n$ is the jth feature of sample $i$ and $v^i \in \mathbb{R}$ is its label
  • The $j$th feature of the $i$th sample produced at hidden layer $l-1$, feeding into hidden layer $l$. $$(y_{l})^{i,j}$$
Suppose now we have $(y_{l-1})^{i,j}$ for every $j$ in some index set $\mathcal{V}_{l-1}$. We aim to produce [one of] the next features $(y_{l})^{i,k}$ for the same sample $i$; namely feature number $k \in \mathcal{V}_l$ not necessarily equal to $j$. The feed-forward neural network (FFN) achieves this in two steps:
  1. We take a set of parameters $\{(W_l)^{j,k} \in \mathbb{R}\}_{j \in \mathcal{V}_{l-1}} \cup \{(b_l)^k\}$ - we may also call these weights and biases - and multiply each to the respective $j$th feature of the $i$th sample at layer $l-1$, before summing. That is, we produce $$ \begin{equation} (z_l)^{i,k} = \sum_{j \in \mathcal{V}_{l-1}}(y_{l-1})^{i,j} (W_l)^{j,k} + (b_l)^k \end{equation} $$ a "preimage" of the $k$th feature of the $i$th sample at layer $l$. Just the preimage thus far, since at step 2...
  2. a smooth, nonlinear transform from $C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}, \mathbb{R})$ is henceforth applied: $$ \begin{equation} (y_l)^{i,k} = f_l((z_l)^{i,k}) \end{equation} $$ producing the desired feature.

Tensors

It is at this point we will specify important details needed to understand the rest of the article. Firstly, we will use Einstein's Summation Convention (ESC) to simplify sums with a repeated index, signifying the summation of the terms over said index. For example, we can rewrite (1) as $$ \begin{equation} (z_l)^{i,k} = (y_{l-1})^{i,j} (W_l)^{j,k} + b^k \end{equation} $$ where the sum is meant to be over $j$, which repeats, and where all terms are zero outside of their domains. This too deserves formalization. Firstly, let's consider $(y_l)$ as a well defined object, namely, a tensor over infinite dimensional vector spaces, but with finite support. Let's clarify, since the $(y_l)^{a,b}$ objects encompass $\mathcal{V}_l$ features for $n$ samples, lets define $$ \begin{align} \bar{\bf x} &= \sum_{i,j} x^{i,j} e_i \otimes e_j \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{Z}} \otimes \mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{Z}} \\ \bar{\bm{v}} &= \sum_i v^i e_i \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{Z}}\\ y_l &= \sum_{i,k} (y_l)^{i,k} e_i \otimes e_k \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{Z}} \otimes \mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{Z}} \\ W_l &= \sum_{j,k} (W_l)^{j,k} e_j \otimes e_k \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{Z}} \otimes \mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{Z}} \\ b_l &= \sum_{i,k} (b_l)^{k} e_k \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{Z}} \end{align} $$
Throughout the article, $\mathbb{V} = \mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{Z}}$ and $\mathbb{V}^{\otimes 2} = \mathbb{V} \otimes \mathbb{V} $ so $b_l, \bar{\bm{v}} \in \mathbb{V}$ and $\bar{\bf x}, y_l,W_l,z_l\in \mathbb{V}^{\otimes 2}$.
We will also make use of Abstract Index Notation (AIN), which is particularly useful when representing the shape of a tensor. For example, we could write $(y_l)^{ab}$ to denote that $y_l \in V \otimes V$, or for a more general example $$ T^{ab}{}_{c}{}^{d}{}_{e} $$ to denote a tensor $T \in V \otimes V \otimes V^* \otimes V \otimes V^*$.
We proceed from the tensorized version of (1) $$ \begin{align} (z_l)^{i,k} e_i \otimes e_k &= ((y_{l-1})^{i,j} (W_l)^{j,k} + (b_l)^{i,k} ) e_i \otimes e_k \\ \implies z_l &= \sum_{i,j,k} (y_{l-1})^{i,j} (W_l)^{j,k} e_i \otimes e_k + \sum_{i,k} (b_l)^{k} e_i \otimes e_k \in V \otimes V \end{align} $$ We also need to transform the $C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}, \mathbb{R})$ transform into one that acts on tensors, that is one from $C^{\infty}(V \otimes V, V \otimes V )$ by defining $$ \begin{equation} y_l = F_l(z_l) = \sum_{i,k} f_l((z_l)^{i,k}) e_i \otimes e_k \end{equation} $$

Contraction, Inner Products, Metric Tensors


The issue with (8) is that is kind of useless; we want to speak in a language of tensors and yet are using the coefficients of the layer features and parameters, instead of working with their tensor representations directly. Using basic linear algebra, one realizes that (8) can be formulated as a matrix product, where the inner product is over the space indexed by $j$. Using tensors however, we see (6) as the image of the metric tensor, or, if one of the $j$ were lowered, as a contraction. Before jumping in, we need to agree on contraction notation; in (AIN) this is straighforward: if it had been the case that our tensors were of mixed $(1,1)$ rank, we could write $$ \begin{equation} (z_l)^a{}_c = (y_{l-1})^a{}_b (W_l)^b{}_c + (b_l)^a{}_c \end{equation} $$ Unfortunately our tensors arent of rank $(1,1)$ so the operation we're looking for is the inner-product, or, contraction against a metric tensor. We should talk briefly about the metric tensor $g_{ab}$ and the inverse metric tensor $ {g}^{ab}$. $\Bbb{V}$ has the canonical orthonormal basis $\{e_i \}$, and is endowed with the canonical inner product $\langle e_i , e_j \rangle = \delta_{ij}$. Such an inner product admits a dual basis for $\Bbb{V}^*$ defined by $\{e^i = \langle \cdot, e_i \rangle\} $. The metric tensors are then given by agreement with the inner products $$\begin{align} g_{ab} = g_{i,j} e^i \otimes e^j = \delta_{ij} e^i \otimes e^j \\ {g}^{ab} = g^{i,j} e_i \otimes e_j = \delta_{ij} e_i \otimes e_j \end{align}$$ Now we can define what it means to contract against the metric $g$
$$ g_{ab} S^{\ldots a \ldots} T^{\ldots b \ldots} = g_{ij} e^i(e_{\alpha}) e^j(e_{\beta}) S^{\mathcal{I}} T^{\mathcal{J}} e_{\mathcal{I} \setminus \mathcal{I}_a}\otimes e_{\mathcal{J} \setminus \mathcal{J}_b} $$
where $\alpha, \beta$ index slots $a, b$ resp. Similarly
$$ g^{ab} S_{\ldots a \ldots} T_{\ldots b \ldots} = g^{ij}e^{\alpha} (e_i) e^{\beta}(e_j) S_{\mathcal{I}} T_{\mathcal{J}} e^{\mathcal{I} \setminus \mathcal{I}_a}\otimes e^{\mathcal{J} \setminus \mathcal{J}_b} $$
Now we can find a suitable tensorial representation for (8) in terms of tensors, not coefficients.
$$ z_l = (y_{l-1})^{i,j}(W_l)^{j,k}e_i \otimes e_k + (b_l)^{k} e_i \otimes e_k= g_{bc}(y_{l-1})^{ab}(W_l)^{cd} + \sum_i e_i \otimes b_l $$

The Model

We now formalize the FNN model $\mathcal{F}$ completely.

  • We will call a layer $l=1...L$ the function $$y_{l-1} \mapsto \mathcal{L}_l(y_{l-1} ; W_l, b_l) = F_l \circ \Gamma_l(y_{l-1}, W_l, b_l)$$ where $$ \begin{equation} \Gamma_{l}({\bf S, T}, \bm{u}) = g_{bc}{\bf S}^{ab}{\bf T}^{cd}+ \sum_i e_i \otimes \bm{u} \end{equation} $$ We will call the neuron $k$ at layer $l$ the function $$ y_{l-1} \mapsto \mathcal{N}_{l,k}(y_{l-1} ; (W_l)^{j,k} e_j , (b_l)^k) = F_l \circ \gamma_{l}(y_{l-1}, (W_l)^{j,k} e_j, (b_l)^k) $$ where $$ \gamma_{l}({\bf S}, \bm{t}, u) = g_{bc}{\bf S}^{ab}\bm{t}^{c}+ u \sum_i e_i $$ Note that $$ \mathcal{N}_{l,k}(y_{l-1} ; (W_l)^{j,k} e_j , (b_l)^k) \otimes e_k = \mathcal{L}_l(y_{l-1}; W_l, b_l) $$ that is, a layer is a sum of neurons.
  • First we start with the input layer , $\mathcal{L}_0$. We define $$y_0 := x $$ for any sample $x \in V \otimes V$ and define $\mathcal{L}_0$ by $x \mapsto y_0$, so $\mathcal{L}_0$ is the identity. It's not strictly a computational layer like defined earlier, however some folks like to speak of an input layer, which refers to this.
  • The hidden layers are $\{\mathcal{L}_l\}_{ l=1\dots L}$
  • The output layer is $$y_L \mapsto \mathcal{L}_{L+1}(y_L; W_{L+1}, b_{L+1}) = F_{L+1} \circ \Gamma(y_{L}, W_{L+1}, b_{L+1})$$ where $F_{L+1}$ is called the activation function.
  • The model $\mathcal{F}$ is defined by $$ \mathcal{F}(x ; \theta) = \mathcal{L}_{L+1}(\cdot ; W_{L+1},b_{L+1}) \circ \ldots \circ \mathcal{L}_{1}(\cdot ; W_{1}, b_1) \circ \mathcal{L_0} (x) $$ where $$\theta = W_1,b_1 \ldots W_{L+1},b_{L+1} $$ are the parameters of the model.

Loss, Empirical Risk

We have so far the ability to compute the output of our parameterized model $y_{L+1} = \mathcal{F}(x;\theta)$.

Let $X,Y$ be the random variables for input features and labels, over the spaces $\mathcal{X} = \mathbb{V}, \mathcal{Y} = \mathbb{R}$. Let $$X,Y \sim \mathcal{P}(X,Y) $$ with pdf $p_{X,Y}(x,y)$. Let $X_i,Y_i$ be the ith feature and label random variables, and assume $$X_i, Y_i \sim_{i.i.d} \mathcal{P}(X,Y)$$ Define a loss function $\mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{V} \to \mathbb{R}:$ $$ w, \hat{w} \mapsto \Delta(w, \hat{w}) $$ Define $$ \begin{align*} T^{[i]} &= \sum_{\alpha} T^{i,\alpha} e_i \otimes e_{\alpha} \\ T^{\rangle i \langle} &= \sum_{\alpha} T^{i,\alpha} e_{\alpha} \\ T^{\langle i \rangle}& = \sum_{\alpha} T^{\alpha} e_i \otimes e_{\alpha} = e_i \otimes T \end{align*} $$ For a batch model $h$ (one defined over $\mathbb{V}^{\otimes 2}$ like ours), the loss at a point $(x,y) \in \mathbb{V} \times \mathbb{R}$ is $$ l(x, y,h) = \Delta(y, h(x^{\langle \mu \rangle})^{\rangle \mu \langle}) $$ The risk is the expected value of the loss over $X \times Y$ $$ R(h) = \mathbb{E}_{X,Y}(l(x,y,h)) = \int l(x,y,h) p_{X,Y}(x,y) dxdy $$ which is intractable, so the empirical risk over a sample $\bm{d}=( \bm{x}, \bm{y})$ is used instead, which, as data size grows, converges to the risk by the LLN: $$ \begin{equation} \hat{R}(\bm{d}, h) = \frac{1}{I}\sum_i l({\bf x}^{\rangle i \langle},y^i,h) = \frac{1}{I}\sum_i l(\bm{x}^{\rangle i \langle},\bm{y}^{\rangle i \langle},h) \to \mathbb{E}_{X,Y}(l(x,y,h)) \end{equation} $$as $I\to\infty$. Empirical Risk Minimization chooses a model space, in this case, $\mathcal{H} = \{\mathcal{F}(\cdot;\theta) \}_{\theta}$ and finds $$ \argmin_{h \in \mathcal{H}}\hat{R}(\bm{d}, h) = \mathcal{F}(\cdot;\theta^*), \theta^* = \argmin_{\theta}\hat{R}({\bm d}, \mathcal{F}(\cdot ; \theta)) $$ so given our sample $\bar{\bm{d}} = (\bar{\bm{x}}, \bar{\bm{v}})$ we find $$ \argmin_{\theta} \hat{R}(\bar{\bm{d}}, \mathcal{F}(\cdot ; \theta)) = \argmin_{\theta}\frac{1}{I}\sum_i l(\bar{\bf x}^{\rangle i \langle},\bar{v}^i, \mathcal{F}(\cdot; \theta)) \\ = \argmin_{\theta}\frac{1}{I}\sum_i \Delta(\bar{v}^i, \mathcal{F}(\bar{\bf x}^{\rangle i \langle \langle i \rangle}; \theta)^{\rangle i \langle }) = \argmin_{\theta}\frac{1}{I}\sum_i\Delta(\bar{v}^i, \mathcal{F}(\bar{\bf x}^{[i]} ; \theta)^{\rangle i \langle} ) $$

Loss Function, Activation Functions

We will need a way to choose the loss function $\Delta$. While we could make an arbitrary choice, we could also use Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) to derive it. Let $$\mathscr{P}=\{\mathcal{P}(X,Y; \theta) : p_{X,Y}(x,y;\theta) = p_{Y|X}(y|x;\theta)p_X(x) = p_0(y;\mathcal{F}(x^{\langle \mu \rangle};\theta)^{\rangle \mu \langle})p_X(x) \}_{\theta \in \Theta }$$ and assume $$X,Y \sim \mathcal{P}(X, Y; {\theta}) \in \mathscr{P} $$ with density $p_{X,Y}(x,y;\theta) = p_0(y;\mathcal{F}(x;\theta))p_X(x)$ for some $\theta$. The sample distribution for the random vector ${\bf D}= ({\bf X}, {\bf Y})$ is then given by the density $$ p_{{\bf D}}(\bm{d};\theta)=_{i.i.d} \prod_i p(x_i, y_i;\theta) = \prod_i p_0(y_i;\mathcal{F}(x_i^{\langle \mu \rangle};\theta)^{\rangle \mu \langle})p_X(x_i) \\ = \prod_i p_0(y_i;\mathcal{F}({\bf x}^{[i]};\theta)^{\rangle i \langle})p_X({\bf x}^{\rangle i \langle}) $$ The likelihood is $$ \mathbb{L}(\theta; {\bf \bar{\bm d}}) = p_{\bf D}({\bf \bar{\bm d}}; \theta) = \prod_i p_0(\bar{v}^i;\mathcal{F}(\bar{\bf x}^{[i]};\theta)^{\rangle i \langle})p_X(\bar{\bf x}^{\rangle i \langle}) $$ Taking the negative log gives $$ -\log(\mathbb{L}(\theta; \bar{\bm d})) = -\sum_i \log(p_X(\bar{\bf x}^{\rangle i \langle})) - \sum_i \log(p_0(\bar{v}^i;\mathcal{F}(\bar{\bf x}^{[i]}; \theta)^{\rangle i \langle})) $$ which we want minimized over $\theta$. To do so, we need only find $$ \begin{equation} \argmin_{\theta}\left(- \sum_i \log(p_0(\bar{v}^i;\mathcal{F}(\bar{\bf x}^{[i]}; \theta)^{\rangle i \langle}))\right) \end{equation} $$ If we set $$\Delta(a, b) := -\log(p_0(a;b)) $$ then $$ (18) = \argmin_{\theta}\left(\sum_i \Delta(\bar{v}^i;\mathcal{F}(\bar{\bf x}^{[i]}; \theta)^{\rangle i \langle})\right) = \argmin_{\theta}\left(\sum_i l(\bar{\bf x}^{\rangle i \langle}, \bar{v}^i, \mathcal{F}(\cdot;\theta)) \right) \\ =\argmin_{\theta} I \hat{R}(\bar{\bm d},\mathcal{F}(\cdot; \theta)) =\argmin_{\theta} \hat{R}(\bar{\bm d},\mathcal{F}(\cdot; \theta))$$ so maximizing the likelihood assuming class $\mathscr{P}$ will also minimize the empirical risk with this loss. By our assumption on the data and $\mathscr{P}$, $$(Y|X=x) \sim \mathcal{P}(Y|X=x; \theta) $$ with $$p_{Y|X=x}(y|x;\theta)=p_0(y;\mathcal{F}(x^{\rangle \mu \langle};\theta)^{\langle \mu \rangle})$$ $$\implies (Y|X=x) \sim \mathcal{P}_0(Y;\mathcal{F}(x^{\rangle \mu \langle};\theta)^{\langle \mu \rangle})$$ for some apriori chosen $\mathcal{P}_0$ with density $p_0$. We now investigate suitable choices for $\mathcal{P}_0$ by the nature of the random variable $(Y|X=x)$:
  • If $(Y|X=x) \in \{0,1\}$ in the case of a binary learning task, then $$\mathcal{P}_0(Y;\hat{y}) = \textbf{Bernoulli}(Y;\hat{y})$$ where $\hat{y} = \mathcal{F}(x^{\langle \mu \rangle};\theta)^{\rangle \mu \langle}$, is a suitable choice (although actually derived from the axioms of probability) with $$p_0(y;\hat{y}) = \hat{y}^y(1-\hat{y})^{1-y} $$ $$\implies \Delta(v, \hat{y})= -\log(p_0(v, \hat{y})) = -\left(v\log(\hat{y}) + (1-v)\log(1-\hat{y})\right) $$ that is, the Binary Cross Entropy (BCE) loss. Such a loss demands our tensor output $\hat{\bm y} =\mathcal{F}({\bf x}; \theta)$ for a sample ${\bf x}$ obey certain properties:
    1. $\hat{\bm y} \in \mathbb{V}$
    2. $\hat{y}^{i} \in [0,1]$
    so that $\Delta$ is well defined, by using $\hat{\bm y}^{\rangle i \langle}$ in place of $\hat{y}$ for each $i$ in the sample. To satisfy (b) we choose the activation $f_{L+1}$ to be the sigmoid activation $$ f_{L+1}: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R} = z \mapsto \frac{1}{1+e^{-z}} $$ To satisfy (a), $$ W_{L+1} \in \mathbb{V} $$ is required.
  • If $(Y|X=x) \in S \subset \mathbb{Z} : \lvert S \rvert < \infty$ more broadly, in the case of categorical learning, then $$ \mathcal{P}_0(Y;{\hat{\bm y}})=\textbf{Categorical}(Y;{\hat{\bm y}}) $$ where ${\hat{\bm y}}=\mathcal{F}(x^{\langle \mu \rangle};\theta)^{\rangle \mu \langle}$, is a suitable choice (although actually derived from the axioms of probability) with $$p_0(y;{\hat{\bm y}})=\prod_c (\bm{\it \hat{y}}^c)^{[y=c]}$$ $$\implies \Delta(v, {\hat{\bm y}})=-\log(p_0(v, {\hat{\bm y}}))=-\sum_c [v=c]\log(\hat{y}^c) $$ that is, the Categorical Cross Entropy (CCE) loss. Such a loss demands our output $\hat{\bf y}=\mathcal{F}({\bf x}, \theta)$ obey certain properties:
    1. $ \text{supp}^{b}(\hat{\bf y}^{ab}) \subset \{e_c\}_{c\leq C} (\implies \text{supp}^b((z_{L+1})^{ab}) \subset \{e_c\}_{c\leq C}) $
    2. $\hat{y}^{i,c} \in [0,1], \sum_c \hat{y}^{i,c} = 1$
    so that $\Delta$ is well defined by using $\hat{\bf y}^{\rangle i \langle}$ in place of $\hat{\bm y}$. To satisfy (b) we choose the activation $F_{L+1}$ to be the softmax activation, a type of tensor activation: $$ F_{L+1}: \mathbb{V}^{\otimes 2} \to \mathbb{V}^{\otimes 2} = z^{i,j}e_i \otimes e_j \mapsto \frac{e^{z^{i,j}}}{\sum_k e^{z^{i,k}}}e_i \otimes e_j $$ At layer $L+1$, there is only one neuron, given by $$\mathcal{N}_{L+1, 1} = \mathcal{L}_{L+1}$$ with $$\text{supp}^b ((W_{L+1})^{ab}) \subset \{e_c\}_{c\leq C} $$ so that $(a)$ is satisfied.
  • If $(Y|X=x) \in \mathbb{R}$ in the case of a regression, then $$ \mathcal{P}_0(Y;\hat{y})=\mathcal{N}(Y;\mu=\hat{y},\sigma^2=1) $$ where $\hat{y} = \mathcal{F}(x^{\langle \mu \rangle};\theta)^{\rangle \mu \langle}$, is a suitable choice with $$p_0(y;\hat{y})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \exp\left({-\frac{(y-\hat{y})^2}{2}}\right)$$ $$\implies \Delta(v, \hat{y})= -\log(p_0(v, \hat{y})) =-\log(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}) + \frac{1}{2}(v - \hat{y})^2 $$ although note to minimize the above, one need only minimize the Mean Squared Error (MSE) term $(v-\hat{y})^2$. Such a loss demands our output $\hat{\bm y}=\mathcal{F}({\bf x}, \theta)$ obey certain properties:
    1. $\hat{\bm y} \in \mathbb{V} $
    so that $\Delta$ is well defined, by using $\hat{\bm y}^{\rangle i \langle}$ in place of $\hat{y}$. To satisfy this requirement $$ W_{L+1} \in \mathbb{V} $$ is required. For regression, the activation is unrestricted and can infact be left out entirely by being set to the identity.

    • Backpropogation

    We can now formulate the backpropogation algorithm, sticking with the language of tensors. Backpropogation will be used to find $$ \theta^* = \argmin_{\theta} \hat{R}(\bar{\bf d}, \mathcal{F}(\cdot; \theta)) $$ via Gradient Descent. Firstly, we find the partial derivative of $\frak{R}$ $$ \partial_{2}\hat{R}(\bar{\bf d}, \mathcal{F}(\cdot; \theta)) $$ Assume as per the previous section we have a loss function $\Delta$ and define the tensorized version by $$ {\bf \Delta}(\bm{v}, T) = \sum_i \Delta(v^i, T^{\rangle i \langle}) $$ so that $$ {\bf \Delta}(\bm{\bar{v}}, \mathcal{F}(\bar{\bf x}; \theta)) = \sum_i \Delta(\bar{v}^i, \mathcal{F}(\bar{\bf x}; \theta)^{\rangle i \langle}) \\ \implies \hat{R}(\bar{\bf d}, \mathcal{F}(\cdot; \theta)) = \frac{1}{I} {\bf \Delta}(\bm{\bar{v}}, \mathcal{F}(\bar{\bf x}; \theta)) $$ Perturb $\mathcal{F}(\cdot; \theta)$ by $h$, then $$ \begin{align*} d \mathcal{F}(\bar{\bf x}; \theta) &= h(\bar{\bf x}) \\ \implies d {\bf \Delta}(\bm{\bar{v}}, \mathcal{F}(\bar{\bf x}; \theta)) &= \partial_2 {\bf \Delta}(\bm{\bar{v}}, \mathcal{F}(\bar{\bf x}; \theta)) [d\mathcal{F}(\bar{\bf x}; \theta)] \\ &= \partial_2 {\bf \Delta}(\bm{\bar{v}}, \mathcal{F}(\bar{\bf x}; \theta)) [h(\bar{\bf x})] \\ \implies d \hat{R}(\bar{\bf d}, \mathcal{F}(\cdot; \theta)) &= \frac{1}{I} \partial_2 {\bf \Delta}(\bm{\bar{v}}, \mathcal{F}(\bar{\bf x}; \theta)) [h(\bar{\bf x})] \\ &= \frac{1}{I} \left( \partial_2 {\bf \Delta}(\bm{\bar{v}}, \mathcal{F}(\bar{\bf x}; \theta)) \circ \delta_{\bar{\bf x}}\right) [h] \\ \implies \partial_2 \hat{R}(\bar{\bf d}, \mathcal{F}(\cdot; \theta)) &= \frac{1}{I} \partial_2 {\bf \Delta}(\bm{\bar{v}}, \mathcal{F}(\bar{\bf x}; \theta)) \circ \delta_{\bar{\bf x}}\\ \end{align*} $$ By the chain rule $$ \partial_{\theta} \hat{R}(\bar{\bf d}, \mathcal{F}(\cdot; \theta)) = \partial_2 \hat{R}(\bar{\bf d}, \mathcal{F}(\cdot; \theta)) \circ \partial_{\theta} \mathcal{F}(\cdot; \theta) \\ = \frac{1}{I}\partial_2 {\bf \Delta}(\bm{\bar{v}}, \mathcal{F}(\bar{\bf x}; \theta)) \circ \delta_{\bar{\bf x}} \circ \partial_{\theta} \mathcal{F}(\cdot; \theta ) $$ $$ \begin{align} &= \frac{1}{I}\partial_2 {\bf \Delta}(\bm{\bar{v}}, \mathcal{F}(\bar{\bf x}; \theta)) \circ \partial_{\theta} \mathcal{F}(\bar{\bf x}; \theta )\\ \end{align} $$ Define $$ \begin{align} S &= \frac{1}{I}{\bf \Delta}(\bar{\bm{v}}, \cdot) \circ F_{L+1} \\ T(\cdot, \theta) &= \Gamma_{L+1}(\cdot, \theta_{L+1}) \circ ... \circ \mathcal{L}_0 \implies \mathcal{F}(\bar{\bf x}; \theta) = F_{L+1} \circ T(\bar{\bf x}, \theta) \end{align} $$ Then $$ \begin{align} \hat{R}(\bar{\bf d}, \mathcal{F}(\cdot; \theta)) &= {\bf \Delta}(\bar{\bm{v}}, \cdot) \circ \mathcal{F}({\bf x}; \theta) \\ & = S \circ T ({\bf x}, \theta) \\ \implies \partial_{\theta} \hat{R}(\bar{\bf d}, \mathcal{F}(\cdot; \theta)) &= DS ({\bf z}_{L+1}) \circ \partial_{2} T(\bar{\bf x},\theta)\\ \end{align} $$ It turns out that combining ${\bf \Delta}$ and $F_{L+1}$ into $S$ results in very elegant derivatives, which we will derive below.
    • If $\bm{\hat y} = \mathcal{F}(\bar{\bf x}, \theta) \in \Bbb{V} $ and $\Delta(\bar{v}^i, \bm{\hat{y}}^{\rangle i \langle}) = -\left(\bar{v}^i\log(\hat{\bm{y}}^{\rangle i \langle}) + (1-\bar{v}^i)\log(\hat{\bm{y}}^{\rangle i \langle})\right) $ in the case of a binary learning task, then tensorizing gives $$ {\bf \Delta}(\bm{\bar{v}}, \hat{\bm{y}}) = -{\bf 1}^T \left(\bar{\bm v} \odot \log(\hat{\bm{y}}) + ({\bf 1}- \bar{\bm v}) \odot \log({\bf 1} - \hat{\bm{y}}) \right) $$ Perburb $\hat{\bm{y}}$ by $d\hat{\bm{y}}$, then $$ \begin{align*} d \log(\bm{\hat{y}} ) &= \bm{\hat{y}}^{-1} \odot d \bm{\hat{y}} \\ d \log(1-\bm{\hat{y}}) &= - ({\bf 1}-\bm{\hat{y}})^{-1} \odot d \bm{\hat{y}} \\ \implies d {\bf \Delta}(\bm{\bar{v}}, \hat{\bm{y}}) &= -{\bf 1}^T \left(\bar{\bm v} \odot (\bm{\hat{y}}^{-1} \odot d \bm{\hat{y}}) + ({\bf 1}-\bar{\bm{v}}) \odot (- ({\bf 1}-\bm{\hat{y}})^{-1} \odot d \bm{\hat{y}}) \right) \\ &= {\bf 1}^T \left(\left(({\bf 1}-\bm{\hat{y}})^{-1} -\bar{\bm{v}} \odot ({\bf 1}-\bm{\hat{y}})^{-1} -\bar{\bm v} \odot \bm{\hat{y}}^{-1} \right) \odot d \bm{\hat{y}}\right) \\ \implies D_2 {\bf \Delta}(\bm{\bar{v}}, \hat{\bm{y}}) &= \bm{h} \mapsto {\bf 1}^T \left(\left(({\bf 1}-\bm{\hat{y}})^{-1} -\bar{\bm{v}} \odot ({\bf 1}-\bm{\hat{y}})^{-1} -\bar{\bm v} \odot \bm{\hat{y}}^{-1} \right) \odot \bm{h}\right) \end{align*} $$ Tensoring the sigmoid activation gives $$ \hat{\bm{y}} = F_{L+1}(\bm{z}_{L+1}) = \sigma(\bm{z}_{L+1}) = ({\bf 1} + \exp(-\bm{z}_{L+1}))^{-1} $$ Perturb $\bm{z}_{L+1}$ by $d\bm{z}_{L+1}$, then $$ \begin{align*} d \exp(-\bm{z}_{L+1}) &= -\exp(-\bm{z}_{L+1}) \odot d\bm{z}_{L+1} \\ \implies d\hat{\bm{y}} = d F_{L+1}(\bm{z}_{L+1}) &=({\bf 1} + \exp(-\bm{z}_{L+1}))^{-2}\odot \exp(-\bm{z}_{L+1})\odot d\bm{z}_{L+1} \\ &= \hat{\bm{y}}^{2}\odot \exp(-\bm{z}_{L+1})\odot d\bm{z}_{L+1} \\ &= \hat{\bm{y}}^{2}\odot ({\bf -1 + 1} + \exp(-\bm{z}_{L+1}))\odot d\bm{z}_{L+1} \\ &= \hat{\bm{y}}^{2}\odot ({\bf - 1} + \hat{\bm{y}}^{-1})\odot d\bm{z}_{L+1} \\ &= \hat{\bm{y}}\odot ({\bf 1} - \hat{\bm{y}})\odot d\bm{z}_{L+1} \\ \end{align*} $$ Now we find $DS (\bm{z}_{L+1}) $ by making the previous preturbations $$ \begin{align*} & d {\bf \Delta}(\bm{\bar{v}},\hat{\bm{y}}) \\ &= {\bf 1}^T \left(\left(({\bf 1}-\bm{\hat{y}})^{-1} -\bar{\bm{v}} \odot ({\bf 1}-\bm{\hat{y}})^{-1} -\bar{\bm v} \odot \bm{\hat{y}}^{-1} \right) \odot d \bm{\hat{y}}\right) \\ &= {\bf 1}^T \left(\left(({\bf 1}-\bm{\hat{y}})^{-1} -\bar{\bm{v}} \odot ({\bf 1}-\bm{\hat{y}})^{-1} -\bar{\bm v} \odot \bm{\hat{y}}^{-1} \right) \odot \left(\hat{\bm{y}}\odot ({\bf 1} - \hat{\bm{y}})\odot d\bm{z}_{L+1} \right)\right) \\ &= {\bf 1}^T \left( \left( \bm{\hat{y}} -\bar{\bm{v}} \odot \bm{\hat{y}} -\bar{\bm v} \odot ({\bf 1} - \bm{\hat{y}}) \right) \odot d\bm{z}_{L+1} \right) \\ &= {\bf 1}^T \left( \left( \bm{\hat{y}} -\bar{\bm{v}} \right) \odot d\bm{z}_{L+1} \right) \\ \implies DS (\bm{z}_{L+1}) &= \bm{h} \mapsto {\bf 1}^T \left( \left( \bm{\hat{y}} -\bar{\bm{v}} \right) \odot \bm{h} \right) \\ &= \langle \cdot,\bm{\hat{y}} -\bar{\bm{v}} \rangle \end{align*} $$
      $$ \therefore \partial_{\theta} \hat{R}(\bar{\bf d}, \mathcal{F}(\cdot; \theta)) = \frac{1}{I} \langle \cdot,\bm{\hat{y}} -\bar{\bm{v}} \rangle \circ \partial_{2} T(\bar{\bf x},\theta) $$
    • If $\hat{\bf y} = \mathcal{F}(\bar{\bf x}; \theta) \in \Bbb{V}^{\otimes 2}$ and $\Delta(\bar{v}^i, \hat{\bf y}^{\rangle i \langle}) = -\sum_c [\bar{v}^i = c] \log((\hat{\bf y}^{\rangle i \langle})^{c})$ in the case of a categorical learning task, then tensorizing gives $$ {\bf \Delta}(\bar{\bm{v}}, \hat{\bf y}) = -{\bf 1}^T \left(\Bbb{X} \odot \log(\hat{\bf y}) \right){\bf 1} $$ where $\Bbb{X} \in \Bbb{V}^{\otimes 2}$ is the one hot tensor of $\bar{\bm{v}}$ defined by $$ \Bbb{X} = \delta_{\bar{v}^i, j} e_i \otimes e_j$$ Perturb $\hat{\bf y}$ by $d \hat{\bf y}$, then $$ \begin{align*} d \log(\hat{\bf y}) &= \hat{\bf y}^{-1} \odot d \hat{\bf y} \\ \implies d (\Bbb{X} \odot \log(\hat{\bf y})) &= \Bbb{X} \odot \hat{\bf y}^{-1} \odot d \hat{\bf y} \\ \implies d {\bf \Delta} (\bar{\bm{v}}, \hat{\bf y}) &= -{\bf 1}^T \left(\Bbb{X} \odot \hat{\bf y}^{-1} \odot d \hat{\bf y} \right){\bf 1} \\ \end{align*} $$ Tensorizing the softmax activation gives $$ \hat{\bf y} = F_{L+1}({\bf z}_{L+1}) = \text{Diag} ((\exp({\bf z}_{L+1}) {\bf 1})^{-1})\exp({\bf z}_{L+1}) $$ Perturb ${\bf z}_{L+1}$ by $d{\bf z}_{L+1}$, then letting $E = \exp({\bf z}_{L+1})$ $$ \begin{align*} d E &= E \odot d{\bf z}_{L+1} \\ \implies d (E {\bf 1}) &= (E \odot d {\bf z}_{L+1}){\bf 1} \\ \implies d (E {\bf 1})^{-1} &= - (E{\bf 1})^{-2} \odot ((E \odot d {\bf z}_{L+1}){\bf 1}) \\ \implies d \hat{\bf y} &= d (\text{Diag}((E{\bf 1})^{-1}))E + \text{Diag}((E{\bf 1})^{-1})d E \\ &= \text{Diag}(- (E{\bf 1})^{-2} \odot ((E \odot d {\bf z}_{L+1}){\bf 1}))E + \text{Diag}((E{\bf 1})^{-1})(E \odot d {\bf z}_{L+1}) \end{align*} $$ Let $A, B$ be arbitrary, and $C=\text{Diag}(c), D=\text{Diag}(d)$ be diagonal. We will use a few facts concerning diagonal matrices and the $\odot $ operator:
      1. $DA = (d{\bf 1}^T) \odot A$
      2. $D(A \odot B) = (DA) \odot B$
      3. $CD = \text{Diag}(c \odot d)$
      Noting that $\hat{\bf y} = \text{Diag}((E{\bf 1})^{-1})E = \text{Diag}(E{\bf 1})^{-1}E \implies E = \text{Diag}(E{\bf 1}) \hat{\bf y}$ $$ \begin{align*} d \hat{\bf y} &= -\text{Diag}( (E{\bf 1})^{-2})\text{Diag}((E \odot d {\bf z}_{L+1}){\bf 1})\text{Diag}(E{\bf 1}) \hat{\bf y} + (\text{Diag}((E{\bf 1})^{-1} )E) \odot d {\bf z}_{L+1}\\ &=-\text{Diag}( (E{\bf 1})^{-1})\text{Diag}( (E \odot d {\bf z}_{L+1}){\bf 1}) \hat{\bf y} + \hat{\bf y} \odot d {\bf z}_{L+1} \\ &=-\text{Diag}( (E{\bf 1})^{-1})(( (E \odot d {\bf z}_{L+1}){\bf 1} {\bf 1}^T)\odot \hat{\bf y}) + \hat{\bf y} \odot d {\bf z}_{L+1} \\ &=-(\text{Diag}( (E{\bf 1})^{-1})(E \odot d {\bf z}_{L+1}){\bf 1} {\bf 1}^T)\odot \hat{\bf y} + \hat{\bf y} \odot d {\bf z}_{L+1} \\ &=-(((\text{Diag}( (E{\bf 1})^{-1})E) \odot d {\bf z}_{L+1}){\bf 1} {\bf 1}^T)\odot \hat{\bf y} + \hat{\bf y} \odot d {\bf z}_{L+1} \\ &=-((\hat{\bf y} \odot d {\bf z}_{L+1}){\bf 1} {\bf 1}^T)\odot \hat{\bf y} + \hat{\bf y} \odot d {\bf z}_{L+1} \\ \end{align*} $$ Now we find $DS({\bf z}_{L+1}) $ by making the previous preturbations $$ \begin{align*} & d {\bf \Delta}(\bm{\bar{v}},\hat{\bf y}) \\ &= d {\bf \Delta}(\bm{\bar{v}}, F_{L+1}({\bf z}_{L+1}) ) = d {\bf \Delta}(\bm{\bar{v}},\hat{\bm{y}}) \\ &= -{\bf 1}^T \left(\Bbb{X} \odot \hat{\bf y}^{-1} \odot d \hat{\bf y} \right){\bf 1} \\ &= {\bf 1}^T \left(\Bbb{X} \odot \hat{\bf y}^{-1} \odot (((\hat{\bf y} \odot d {\bf z}_{L+1}){\bf 1} {\bf 1}^T)\odot \hat{\bf y} - \hat{\bf y} \odot d {\bf z}_{L+1} ) \right){\bf 1} \\ &= {\bf 1}^T \left(\Bbb{X} \odot ((\hat{\bf y} \odot d {\bf z}_{L+1}){\bf 1} {\bf 1}^T) - \Bbb{X} \odot d {\bf z}_{L+1} \right){\bf 1} \\ &= {\bf 1}^T \left(\Bbb{X} \odot ((\hat{\bf y} \odot d {\bf z}_{L+1}){\bf 1} {\bf 1}^T)\right) {\bf 1} - {\bf 1}^T \left( \Bbb{X} \odot d {\bf z}_{L+1} \right){\bf 1} \\ &= {\bf 1}^T \left(\hat{\bf y} \odot d {\bf z}_{L+1} \right) {\bf 1} - {\bf 1}^T \left( \Bbb{X} \odot d {\bf z}_{L+1} \right){\bf 1} \\ &= {\bf 1}^T \left((\hat{\bf y} - \Bbb{X}) \odot d {\bf z}_{L+1} \right) {\bf 1} \end{align*} $$ where the second to last line makes use of the identity $(\Bbb{X} \odot (A {\bf 1 1}^T)){\bf 1} = A{\bf 1} $ for any $A$ and 1-hot $\Bbb{X}$. Thus $$ \begin{align*} DS({\bf z}_{L+1}) &= {\bf h} \mapsto {\bf 1}^T \left((\hat{\bf y} - \Bbb{X}) \odot {\bf h} \right) {\bf 1} \\ &= \langle \cdot,{\bf \hat{y}} -\Bbb{X} \rangle \end{align*} $$
      $$ \therefore \partial_{\theta} \hat{R}(\bar{\bf d}, \mathcal{F}(\cdot; \theta)) = \frac{1}{I} \langle \cdot,{\bf \hat{y}} -\Bbb{X} \rangle \circ \partial_2 T(\bar{\bf x},\theta) $$
    • If $\bm{\hat y} = \mathcal{F}(\bar{\bf x}, \theta) \in \Bbb{V} $ and $\Delta(\bar{v}^i, \bm{\hat{y}}^{\rangle i \langle}) = (\bar{v}^i - \bm{\hat{y}}^{\rangle i \langle})^2$ in the case of a regression task, then tensorizing gives $$ {\bf \Delta}(\bm{\bar{v}}, \hat{\bm{y}}) = {\bf 1}^T(\bm{\bar{v}}-\hat{\bm{y}})^2{\bf 1} $$ Perturbing $\hat{\bm{y}}$ by $d\hat{\bm{y}}$ gives $$ \begin{align*} d{\bf \Delta}(\bm{\bar{v}}, \hat{\bm{y}}) &= {\bf 1}^T(-2(\bm{\bar{v}}-\hat{\bm{y}}) \odot d\hat{\bm{y}}){\bf 1} \\ &= -2{\bf 1}^T((\bm{\bar{v}}-\hat{\bm{y}}) \odot d\hat{\bm{y}}){\bf 1} \\ \implies D_2{\bf \Delta}(\bm{\bar{v}}, \hat{\bm{y}}) &= \bm{h} \mapsto -2{\bf 1}^T((\bm{\bar{v}}-\hat{\bm{y}}) \odot h){\bf 1} \\ &= 2\langle \cdot, \hat{\bm{y}}- \bm{\bar{v}} \rangle \end{align*} $$ If $F_{L+1}$ is the identity, we further conclude $$ DS(\bm{z}_{L+1}) = 2\langle \cdot, \hat{\bm{y}}- \bm{\bar{v}} \rangle $$
      $$ \therefore \partial_{\theta} \hat{R}(\bar{\bf d}, \mathcal{F}(\cdot; \theta)) = \frac{2}{I} \langle \cdot, \hat{\bm{y}}- \bm{\bar{v}} \rangle \circ \partial_2 T(\bar{\bf x},\theta) $$
    We are now remained with finding $\partial_{\theta} \hat{R}(\bar{\bf d}; \mathcal{F}(\cdot; \theta)) $ which we can do recursively, making up the backpropogation procedure. Firstly $$ \begin{align*} \partial_{\theta_{L+1}} \hat{R}(\bar{\bf d}; \mathcal{F}(\cdot; \theta)) &= DS({\bf z}_{L+1}) \circ \partial_{2}\Gamma_{L+1}({\bf y}_L, \theta_{L+1}) \end{align*} $$ Let, for $l=L\ldots 1$ $$ \begin{align*} U_l(\cdot, \theta) :&= S \circ \Gamma_{L+1}(\cdot, \theta_{L+1}) \circ F_{L} \circ ... \circ F_{l+1} \circ \Gamma_{l+1}(\cdot, \theta_{l+1}) \\ \implies \hat{R}(\bar{\bf d}; \mathcal{F}(\cdot; \theta)) &= \left( U_l(\cdot, \theta) \circ F_l \circ \Gamma_l(\cdot, \theta_l) \circ \ldots \circ \mathcal{L}_0 \right)(\bar{\bf x}) \end{align*} $$$$ \begin{align} \implies \begin{cases} \\ \partial_{W_l} \hat{R}(\bar{\bf d}; \mathcal{F}(\cdot; \theta)) &= \partial_{1} U_l({\bf y}_l, \theta) \circ DF_l({\bf z}_l) \circ \partial_{2}\Gamma_l({\bf y}_{l-1}, \theta_l) \\ \partial_{b_l} \hat{R}(\bar{\bf d}; \mathcal{F}(\cdot; \theta)) &= \partial_{1} U_l({\bf y}_l, \theta) \circ DF_l({\bf z}_l) \circ \partial_{3}\Gamma_l({\bf y}_{l-1}, \theta_l) \end{cases} \end{align} $$ where $$ \begin{align} U_{l}(\cdot, \theta) &= \begin{cases} S \circ \Gamma_{L+1}(\cdot, \theta_{L+1}) & l=L \\ U_{l+1}(\cdot, \theta) \circ F_{l+1} \circ \Gamma_{l+1}(\cdot, \theta_{l+1}) & l<L \end{cases} \\ \implies \partial_{1} U_l({\bf y}_l, \theta) &=\begin{cases} DS({\bf z}_{L+1}) \circ \partial_1 \Gamma_{L+1}({\bf y}_{L}, \theta_{L+1}) & l=L\\ \partial_{1} U_{l+1}({\bf y}_{l+1}, \theta) \circ DF_{l+1}({\bf z}_{l+1}) \circ \partial_1 \Gamma_{l+1}({\bf y}_l, \theta_{l+1}) & l<L \end{cases} \end{align} $$ So the recursion is well posed. Finally $$ \partial_{\theta} \hat{R}(\bar{\bf d}; \mathcal{F}(\cdot; \theta)) \\ = \left( (dW_1, db_1 \ldots dW_{L+1}, db_{L+1}) \mapsto \sum_l \partial_{W_l} \hat{R}(\bar{\bf d}; \mathcal{F}(\cdot; \theta)) [d W_l] + \sum_l \partial_{b_l} \hat{R}(\bar{\bf d}; \mathcal{F}(\cdot; \theta)) [d b_l] \right) $$ allowing the full computation of $\partial_{\theta}\hat{R}(\bar{\bf d}, \mathcal{F}(\cdot;\theta))$. A few derivatives will need to be computed. We will write the maps using their tensor identifications, as that will allow an alternative way to not only compose maps but also compute their adoints, which we will need when finding the gradient.

    The Hom-Tensor Identification

    A word on the canonical identification: if we have a map $T \in \text{Hom}(V, W)$ where $V$ is a tensor product space with basis $\{e_{\mathcal{I}}\} $, and the basis for $V^*$ is induced by the standard inner product $\langle e_{\mathcal{I}} , e_{\mathcal{J}} \rangle = \delta_{\mathcal{I}, \mathcal{J}} $ giving $\{(e_{\mathcal{I}})' = \langle \cdot , e_{\mathcal{I}} \rangle \}$, then using $$ \text{Hom}(V,W) \cong W\otimes V^* \\ (\bigotimes V_i)^* \cong \bigotimes V_i^* $$ we have the identification
    $$ \phi: T \mapsto T(e_{\mathcal{I}}) \otimes (e_{\mathcal{I}})' \mapsto T(e_{\mathcal{I}}) \otimes e^{\mathcal{I}} = \phi(T) $$
    Composition with $\phi(S) = S(f_{\mathcal J}) \otimes f^{\mathcal{J}}$ is then given by the slot-wise contraction of the input slot $e^{\mathcal{I}}$ of $\phi(T)$ with the output slot $S(f_{\mathcal{J}})$ of $S$ $$ \phi(T \circ S) = T(e_{\mathcal{I}}) \otimes e^{\mathcal{I}}(S(f_{\mathcal{J}})) \otimes f^{\mathcal{J}} = e^{\mathcal{I}}(S(f_{\mathcal{J}})) T(e_{\mathcal{I}}) \otimes f^{\mathcal{J}} = (\phi(T))^{a}{}_b (\phi(S))^{b}{}_c $$
    • $DF_l({\bf z}_l)$:
      Perturb ${\bf z}_l$ by $d {\bf z}_l$, then $$ \begin{align*} dF_l({\bf z}_l) = f_l'((z_l)^{i,j})d(z_l)^{i,j}e_i \otimes e_j &= F_l'({\bf z}_l) \odot d{\bf z}_l \\ \implies DF_l({\bf z}_l) = {\bf h} \mapsto F_l'({\bf z}_l) \odot {\bf h} \end{align*} $$ where $F_l'({\bf z}_l) := f_l'((z_l)^{i,j})e_i \otimes e_j$ $$ \begin{align*} \implies \phi(DFl({\bf z}_l)) &= F'_l({\bf z}_l) \odot (e_i \otimes e_j) \otimes e^i \otimes e^j = f'_l(({z}_l)^{i,j}) \otimes e_i \otimes e_j \otimes e^i \otimes e^j \\ \end{align*} $$
    • $\partial_{2}\Gamma_l({\bf y}_{l-1}, \theta_l) $:
      Contraction of two tensors against the metric, and $\otimes$, are both bilinear so $$ \begin{align*} \partial_{W_l} \Gamma_l({\bf y}_{l-1}, \theta_l) =&\quad dW_l \mapsto g_{bc}({\bf y}_{l-1})^{ab}(dW_l)^{cd} \\ \xmapsto[]{\phi}&\quad g_{bc}({\bf y}_{l-1})^{ab}(e_i \otimes e_j)^{cd} \otimes e^i \otimes e^j\\ =&\quad ({y}_{l-1})^{x,i} e_{x} \otimes e_j \otimes e^i \otimes e^j\\ \partial_{b_l} \Gamma_l({\bf y}_{l-1}, \theta_l) =&\quad db_l \mapsto \sum_i e_i \otimes db_l \\ \xmapsto[]{\phi}&\quad \sum_{i} e_i \otimes e_j \otimes e^j \end{align*} $$
    • $\partial_{1}\Gamma_l({\bf y}_{l-1},\theta_l) $:
      $$ \begin{align*} \partial_{1} \Gamma_l({\bf y}_{l-1}, \theta_l) =&\quad d{\bf y}_{l-1} \mapsto g_{bc}(d{\bf y}_{l-1})^{ab}(W_l)^{cd} \\ \xmapsto[]{\phi}&\quad g_{bc}(e_i \otimes e_j)^{ab}(W_l)^{cd} \otimes e^i \otimes e^j\\ =&\quad (W_l)^{jx} e_i \otimes e_x \otimes e^i \otimes e^j \end{align*} $$
    Now we can calculate the full derivative of each parameter.

    Adjunction

    The gradient is technically the adjoint of the derivative $$ \nabla f(x) = (Df(x))^{\dagger} $$ Since we will express all derivatives as tensors, we need to quickly mention the suitable 'adjunction' operator for tensors: if $T \in \text{Hom}(V, W)$ where $V$ has basis $\{e_{\mathcal{I}}\}$, $W$ has basis $\{f_{\mathcal{I}}\}$, and both spaces are endowed with the standard products $\langle e_{\mathcal{I}}, e_{\mathcal{J}}\rangle = \delta_{\mathcal{I}, \mathcal{J}}$ and $\langle f_{\mathcal{I}}, f_{\mathcal{J}}\rangle = \delta_{\mathcal{I}, \mathcal{J}}$ , and $V^*, W^*$ have the dual bases induced from these inner products, then $$ \phi(T^{\dagger}) = \langle f_{\mathcal{J}}, T(e_{\mathcal{I}}) \rangle e_{\mathcal{I}} \otimes f^{\mathcal{J}} $$ Also $$ \phi(T) = T(e_{\mathcal{I}}) \otimes e^{\mathcal{I}} \\ = \langle f_{\mathcal{J}}, T(e_{\mathcal{I}}) \rangle f_{\mathcal{J}} \otimes e^{\mathcal{I}} $$ Thus 'adunction' on tensors can be defined by
    $$ \phi(T^a{}_b)^{\dagger} := T^b{}_{a} = \phi(T^{\dagger}) $$

    The Gradients

    In practice, solving for $\theta^* = \argmin \hat{R}(\bar{\bf d}; \mathcal{F}(\cdot; \theta))$ is infeasible for many reasons, two important ones being the many flat regions of the objective $\hat{R}$ and the large matrix inversions that would have to be computed. However, now that we've obtained the partial derivatives of $\hat{R}$ at $(\bar{\bf d}; \mathcal{F}(\cdot; \theta))$ we can subtract the gradient from $\theta$ to move towards the optimal point. The procedure to find the gradients will be illustrated. Firstly, some shorthands; let $l$ be a layer index and denote $$ \begin{align*} {\bm{\xi}}_l &= \phi(\partial_{1} U_l({\bf y}_l, \theta)) &= ({\xi}_l)^{i,j} e^i \otimes e^j\\ {\bm{\xi}}_z &= \phi(\partial_{1} U_l({\bf y}_l, \theta) \circ DF_l({\bf z}_l)) \\ {\bm{\delta}}_w &= \phi(\partial_{W_l} \hat{R}(\bar{\bf d}; \mathcal{F}(\cdot; \theta))) \\ {\bm{\delta}}_b &= \phi(\partial_{b_l} \hat{R}(\bar{\bf d}; \mathcal{F}(\cdot; \theta)))\\ \bm{\epsilon}_w &= \phi(\partial_{W_l}\Gamma_l({\bf y}_{l-1}, \theta_l)) &= ({y}_{l-1})^{x,i} e_{x} \otimes e_j \otimes e^i \otimes e^j \\ \bm{\epsilon}_b &= \phi(\partial_{b_l}\Gamma_l({\bf y}_{l-1}, \theta_l)) &= \sum_{i,j} e_i \otimes e_j \otimes e^j \\ \bm{\epsilon}_z &= \phi(DF_l({\bf z}_l)) & = f'_l(({z}_l)^{i,j}) \otimes e_i \otimes e_j \otimes e^i \otimes e^j\\ \bm{\epsilon}_{l-1} &= \phi(\partial_{1} \Gamma_l({\bf y}_{l-1}; \theta_l)) &= (W_l)^{j,x} e_i \otimes e_x \otimes e^i \otimes e^j \\ \end{align*} $$ By the preceding work we can find the gradient tensors for each parameter $$ \begin{align*} \bm{\delta}_w &= \phi \left( \partial_{1} U_l({\bf y}_l, \theta) \circ DF_l({\bf z}_l) \circ \partial_{W_l}\Gamma_l({\bf y}_{l-1}, \theta_l) \right) \\ &= (\bm{\xi}_z){}_{ab} (\bm{\epsilon}_w)^{ab}{}_{cd} \\ &= (\xi_z)_{p,q} (y_{l-1})^{p,i} \otimes e^i \otimes e^q \\ \implies \bm{\delta}_w^{\dagger} &= (\xi_z)_{p,q} (y_{l-1})^{p,i} \otimes e_i \otimes e_q \\ &= (\xi_z^{\dagger})^{p,q} (y_{l-1}^T)^{i,p} \otimes e_i \otimes e_q \\ &= g_{bc}({\bf y}_{l-1}^T)^{ab}(\bm{\xi}_z^{\dagger})^{bc} \\ \\ \bm{\delta}_b &= \phi \left( \partial_{1} U_l({\bf y}_l, \theta) \circ DF_l({\bf z}_l) \circ \partial_{b_l}\Gamma_l({\bf y}_{l-1}, \theta_l) \right) \\ &= (\bm{\xi}_z){}_{ab} (\bm{\epsilon}_b)^{ab}{}_{c} \\ &= \sum_p (\xi_z)_{p,q} e^q \\ \implies \bm{\delta}_b^{\dagger} &= \sum_p (\xi_z)_{p,q} e_q \\ &= \sum_p (\xi_z^{\dagger})^{p,q} e_q \\ &= {\bm{1}}_{a}(\bm{\xi}_z^{\dagger})^{ab} \\ \end{align*} $$ where $$ \begin{align*} \bm{\xi}_z &= (\bm{\xi}_l)_{ab}(\bm{\epsilon}_z)^{ab}{}_{cd}\\ &= (\xi_l)_{i,j} f_l'((z_l)^{i,j}) e^i \otimes e^j \\ \implies \bm{\xi}_z^{\dagger} &= (\xi_l)_{i,j} f_l'((z_l)^{i,j}) e_i \otimes e_j \\ &= (\xi_l^{\dagger})^{i,j} f_l'((z_l)^{i,j}) e_i \otimes e_j \\ &= \bm{\xi}_l^{\dagger} \odot F_l'({\bf z}_l) \\ \end{align*} $$ and $$ \begin{align*} \bm{\xi}_{l-1} &= \phi(\partial_1 U_{l-1}({\bf y}_{l-1}, \theta)) = \phi(\partial_{1} U_{l}({\bf y}_{l}, \theta) \circ DF_{l}({\bf z}_{l}) \circ \partial_1 \Gamma_{l}({\bf y}_{l-1}, \theta_l))\\ &= (\bm{\xi}_l)_{ab} (\bm{\epsilon}_z)^{ab}{}_{cd} (\bm{\epsilon}_{l-1})^{cd}{}_{ef}\\ &= (\bm{\xi}_z)_{cd} (\bm{\epsilon}_{l-1})^{cd}{}_{ef}\\ &= (\xi_z)_{p,q} (W_l)^{j, q} e^p \otimes e^j \\ \implies \bm{\xi}_{l-1}^{\dagger} & = (\xi_l)_{p,q} (W_l)^{j, q} e_p \otimes e_j \\ & = (\xi_l^{\dagger})^{p,q} (W_l^T)^{q, j} e_p \otimes e_j \\ & = g_{bc}(\bm{\xi_l}^{\dagger})^{ab} (W_l^T)^{cd} \end{align*} $$
