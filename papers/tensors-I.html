Tensors I:
Contruction

Tensors are often thought of as multidimensional arrays, especially in programming with NumPy and general Machine Learning tasks. The reader may also be aware of their usage in physics (general relativity) and engineering. In this article we explore the motivation for creating and using tensors in a way that reintroduces structure often glossed over in introductory examples.

The Multilinear Question

Something larger

All is not lost, the student has gained wisdom\mathscr{wisdom}wisdom. He realizes that the key issue with his *even more ambitious approach* is that elements in XXX are, even though the quotient is a set quotient and not a vector space quotient, still just elements of the form [(v,w)][(v,w)][(v,w)]. Sure they may be equivalence classes of a more flexible nature, but if every linear combination of vectors collapses to a single element, then the image of TfT_fTf​ on that vector is still going to be rank ≤1\leq 1≤1, for the example fff from earlier. It would be nice if they didn't just collapse to a single element, because then, we could say Tf([(e1,e1)])+Tf([(e2,e2)])≠T([(v,w)])T_f([(e_1, e_1)]) + T_f([(e_2, e_2)]) \neq T([(v, w)])Tf​([(e1​,e1​)])+Tf​([(e2​,e2​)])=T([(v,w)]) for any [(v,w)]∈X[(v, w)] \in X[(v,w)]∈X.
